Effective accelerationism[1] is one of the most evil paths being
taken in the 21st century. I don't have much hope in humanity any
longer. I'd say "good riddance", but I and countless others will
needlessly suffer from the actions of the greedy elite, as we do
now. For decades we have been destroying our planet in the name
of profit, but what do you and I have to do with it? Sure, we may
have contributed, but none of it reached the scale of what the US
military and various corporations have done. Greed is in inborn
disease. Many want more - more money, more pleasure, and more life.
Some see the defeat of aging as the best thing that could happen
to humanity. But I vehemently disagree. Immortality and/or much
longer lives will take away what makes us human. But we may not
even reach that if we really will place artificial intelligence
in positions of power. AI is rather dumb at the moment, but the 
tech develops in a terrifyingly fast manner. Right now it's
not getting dumber, faster, but it certainly is getting more
dangerous. For a while I believed that AI will collapse before
it becomes "something", but I am not so sure anymore. My land as
I know it will disappear, and I don't know if I'll be there to
mourn it. My mother refuses to make art because she doesn't want
to write elegies and reflect her own misery. I refuse to adopt
that view, but I don't have much hope for humanity. I've longed
for death for so long, but not like this. This is a slow and
painful extinction. I have always hated humanity, but this is
a shameful way to die. We have built ourselves and we will be our
destroyers. I would rather die under divine wrath, fighting
a battle to prove to myself that my faith will save me. Yet the
world is run by devils on Earth. How devilish it is to be
human, after all. Folks spin up tales of inhuman evil to explain
away what really is just our own creation. Even if there are
no Satanic child-eating cults, there is still the upper-class
and its exploitation of those "below" them, those planted in
terminal poverty, and those who resist them. I used to think
a revolution was pointless, that if I raised my voice I will
be killed only for nothing to happen next. But I now believe
that I would rather fight and lose for the sake of having done it,
rather than lose having never tried, because change is
possible, though I am above all a believer in the natural order.
The world is built upon cycles. Change will happen. Change is
inevitable. We may bring it about sooner if we willed it.
They will try to crush us, but we will resist, and we will
rebuild ourselves again.
